## Carrier profiles

=== "Ryanair (RYAAY)"

    **Rank 2 · Score 90.7 · 6.1x EV/EBITDA · the expensive one**

    | Strengths | Weaknesses |
    |---|---|
    | Only net-cash carrier (-0.5x net debt/EBITDA) | Most expensive on every multiple |
    | Collects in 1 day — no agent or interline lag | Cash ratio fell 0.48 → 0.32 over four years |
    | Best current ratio (0.90x) | DPO collapsed 45.5 → 18.5 days, halving its float |
    | Absorbs -39% before burning | March year-end sits at the winter cash trough |

    **What to watch.** The DPO compression is the live story: a 27-day swing in four years is the single largest working-capital move in the group, and it is what took the cash cycle from -43 to -17 days. If it continues, the model's structural funding advantage keeps eroding.

    **What would change the read.** A return to net debt, or the cash ratio dropping below ~0.20 while capex commitments stay high.

=== "IAG (IAG.L)"

    **Rank 1 · Score 92.9 · 3.6x EV/EBITDA · best on the composite**

    | Strengths | Weaknesses |
    |---|---|
    | Best or near-best on four of five composite metrics | Current ratio 0.70x, third of four |
    | Largest cash balance (€7.42bn) | €7.0bn of lease liabilities inside EV |
    | Deepest shock absorbed (-43%) | Rank 1 flips to rank 2 under equal weights |
    | Best cash ratio (0.40x); 16.7 months at -70% | DSO improved but DPO fell 57.1 → 42.7 days |

    **What to watch.** It ranks first yet trades at 60% of Ryanair's multiple — the position on this site's [scatter](risk-vs-value.md) closest to the empty "resilient and cheap" quadrant. Whether that is an opportunity or a correct discount for something this analysis does not model (network, regulatory, ownership) is exactly the question the framework hands off to you.

    **What would change the read.** A cash ratio falling toward the 0.30 peer median, or leverage rising from 0.8x net debt/EBITDA.

=== "Air France-KLM (AF.PA)"

    **Rank 3 · Score 62.2 · 2.4x EV/EBITDA · the mixed one**

    | Strengths | Weaknesses |
    |---|---|
    | Ties the leaders on both stress metrics | Cash cycle has gone to zero (-0.7 days) |
    | Highest FCF yield (19.1%) | Worst current ratio (0.62x) |
    | Absorbs -41% before burning | €10.8bn of leases — 70% of total debt |
    | Cheapest on EV/EBITDA and P/E | Equity is a 22% sliver of a €14.6bn enterprise |

    **What to watch.** The 62.2 score is genuinely mixed, not middling: component scores are 100 on the stress metrics and 0 on both the current ratio and the cash cycle. The 2.1x P/E is mostly a leverage readout — a thin equity slice on a levered enterprise produces a violent multiple. Read the enterprise multiples.

    **What would change the read.** The CCC turning positive (it was +0.9 in FY2023, so it has crossed before), or the cash ratio falling below ~0.20 while the lease book stays this size.

=== "Lufthansa (LHA.DE)"

    **Rank 4 · Score 13.8 · 3.2x EV/EBITDA · the outlier**

    | Strengths | Weaknesses |
    |---|---|
    | Second-best current ratio (0.81x) | Cash ratio 0.06x — a fifth of the peer median |
    | Longest DPO in the group (46.1 days) | Breaks at -24%, two-thirds the group's shock depth |
    | | Only negative FCF yield (-4.6%) |
    | | 1.5 months of cash at -70%; 4.3 at -40% |
    | | Smallest cash balance (€1.16bn) |

    **What to watch.** The only carrier whose survival depends on the unobservable cost-split assumption — its runway at -40% moves 3.0 → 7.4 months across a 45–65% variable share. It ranks fourth under every weighting and every assumption tested, 48 points clear of third, yet trades *above* Air France-KLM. That gap between the balance sheet and the multiple is the sharpest disagreement on the site.

    **What would change the read.** Rebuilding cash toward a 0.15–0.20 cash ratio, or FCF turning positive. Note that the model excludes external liquidity, and Lufthansa's access to it is arguably the best of the four — 2020 is the evidence.
